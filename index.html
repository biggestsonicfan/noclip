Sega Model 2

Interactive 3D explorer

Drop a ROM set and the viewer works out which game it is from the program ROM inside it. Five are supported:

  • Sonic The Fighters — stages, character rigs and motions. Built from that game's own program EPROMs, epr-19001–epr-19004, and the mask ROMs mpr-19005–mpr-19020, which MAME shares with the parent set Sonic Championship (schamp). Drop sfight.zip on its own, or schamp.zip on its own, or both together if your set is split across the two.
  • The House of the Dead (hotdo.zip) — the finished game: its four chapters as eighteen stages, 7477 models under their development names, and 94 enemy bodies playing the 674 motions baked for their joint counts. Revision A (hotd.zip) is recognised as well, and so is a merged archive carrying both — a set is told apart by the chips inside it, not by the name of the zip.
  • The House of the Dead (prototype) (hotdp.zip) — the Model 2C prototype the finished game grew out of, read the same way: its two playable stages, its own models and 68 bodies with 507 motions.
  • Daytona USA (daytona.zip) — the only one here on the original Model 2 board rather than the 2A/2B the rest run on, and the only one that shipped in eight builds. All eight are recognised, and a merged archive is read as all of them at once: the panel grows a Build picker and swapping is instant. Two of them are distinct — the 1993 Deluxe version has 2822 table entries and every build after it 3190, byte for byte the same table in all seven. Out comes the grid of stock cars in their liveries, the courses' track sections and the scenery along them, textured from the course's own bank and shaded through the colour tables the game computes at boot. Its courses come out too: each is a 16x16 grid of 128-unit blocks and one model a block, drawn where it lies, so the Stages tab has the Three-Seven Speedway oval, Dinosaur Canyon, Seaside Street Galaxy and the striped test square the fourth table holds. There are no animations — this game carries no rig of any kind — and the cars do not move.
  • Fighting Vipers (fvipers.zip) — experimental. Sixteen arenas and 5413 table entries come out of it, because this game and Sonic The Fighters are built on the same Sega library and the stage records, the model table and the texture and colour pipelines all carried across. What is its own has not been found: an arena stands still, since the object list its record points at is unread; the arenas have no names, so each is shown by the number in its own record; and there are no character rigs or motions, so no Animation tab.

Sega Model 2
A body is a tree of parts from 0x94E20, each a model hung off its parent and turned by one joint's three angles. A motion is those angles baked for every frame, with the root's position in front, and holds no joint count of its own: its length gives one, so each body lists the motions written for as many joints as it has. The game draws a motion on the spot and moves the enemy along its path separately; root travel shows that path.
A motion id indexes offset_list_motions at 0x6400004. Its record holds 60 channels — 20 objects × 3 axes — of constant, linear or cubic-Hermite keyframes; twelve objects are joint angles and eight are the points the pose is solved towards. The rig is not a plain chain: the chest and head aim at a target, and each limb is a two-bone IK chain reaching for one, exactly as calc_rob_angle_cont drives the coprocessor. See Animation.
