Bring your own ROM set

The viewer works out which game a set is from the program ROM inside it, so the zip can be called anything.

What each set gives you
  • Sonic The Fighters — the one it goes deepest on. Every arena, running at the board's 60 Hz with the objects its own record asks for, and the Flying Carpet and Canyon Cruise's boat flying their courses; all 5103 models; and the whole cast of 34 on their own rigs, playing the game's 518 motions with sway chains, Tails' tails and afterimage trails. Built from that game's own program EPROMs, epr-19001–epr-19004, and the mask ROMs mpr-19005–mpr-19020, which MAME shares with the parent set Sonic Championship (schamp). Drop sfight.zip on its own, or schamp.zip on its own, or both together if your set is split across the two.
  • The House of the Dead (hotdo.zip) — the finished game: its four chapters as eighteen stages, 7477 models under their development names, and 94 enemy bodies playing the 674 motions baked for their joint counts. Revision A (hotd.zip) is recognised as well, and so is a merged archive carrying both — a set is told apart by the chips inside it, not by the name of the zip.
  • The House of the Dead (prototype) (hotdp.zip) — the Model 2C prototype the finished game grew out of, read the same way: its two playable stages, its own models and 68 bodies with 507 motions.
  • Daytona USA (daytona.zip) — the only one here on the original Model 2 board rather than the 2A/2B the rest run on, and the only one that shipped in eight builds. All eight are recognised, and a merged archive is read as all of them at once: the panel grows a Build picker and swapping is instant. Two of them are distinct — the 1993 Deluxe version has 2822 table entries and every build after it 3190, byte for byte the same table in all seven. Out comes the grid of stock cars in their liveries, the courses' track sections and the scenery along them, textured from the course's own bank and shaded through the colour tables the game computes at boot. Its courses come out too: each is a 16x16 grid of 128-unit blocks and one model a block, drawn where it lies, so the Stages tab has the Three-Seven Speedway oval, Dinosaur Canyon, Seaside Street Galaxy and the striped test square the fourth table holds, under the tile chip's panorama of sky. What stands along them moves the way the board moves it — the slot machine's reels, the dice, the wind farm, the boat, the gulls and the horses, which bolt if you fly at them — and a picker draws a course as a race, in time attack or in the ending. A Cabinet switch picks the colour tables the test menu's setting would. The cars carry no rig and do not move, so there is no Animation tab.
  • Fighting Vipers (fvipers.zip) — experimental. Sixteen arenas and 5413 table entries come out of it, because this game and Sonic The Fighters are built on the same Sega library and the stage records, the model table and the texture and colour pipelines all carried across. What is its own has not been found: an arena stands still, since the object list its record points at is unread; the arenas have no names, so each is shown by the number in its own record; and there are no character rigs or motions, so no Animation tab.

        A body is a tree of parts from 0x94E20, each a model hung
        off its parent and turned by one joint's three angles. A motion is
        those angles baked for every frame, with the root's position in front,
        and holds no joint count of its own: its length gives one, so each
        body lists the motions written for as many joints as it has. The game
        draws a motion on the spot and moves the enemy along its path
        separately; root travel shows that path.
      

      

        A fighter is the record at 0x200620C: sixteen part
        models and the skeleton they hang on. A motion id indexes the table
        at 0x2120004, keyframes in the format Sonic The Fighters
        kept. The pose is solved on one standard rig for everyone
        (0x296A0) and the parts are then placed along the
        fighter's own skeleton, as the board draws them. The
        opponent (off by default) stands where a round starts the
        other, ahead of the fighter: the same fighter unless picked, playing
        the same motion, or the hit, counter, guard or knock-down motion the
        game gives the one the fighter's strike lands on (from the frame it
        lands), or the thrown one's with a throw. Behind stands it at the fighter's back instead,
        where it is once the fight has turned a fighter round: a motion does
        not say which, so turning motions and back-turned ones want it
        there. Each head then turns
        toward the other's on every frame. The game does that only when the
        fight state allows, which the explorer does not track, so with it on,
        a head can turn where the game's doesn't.
      

      

        A fighter is the record at 0x640D2B0: sixteen part
        models and the skeleton they hang on. A motion id indexes the table
        at 0x6C00004, keyframes in the format Sonic The Fighters
        kept. The pose is Virtua Fighter 2's: solved on one standard rig for
        everyone (0x2C194), then the parts placed along the
        fighter's own skeleton. The roster ends in Sonic, drawn at 0.56 of
        the motion's height (B.M. at 1.1), two untextured dummies and Jane in
        other clothes. The opponent can play the reaction to the
        fighter's move, as in Virtua Fighter 2. With it on, each head
        turns toward the other's every frame, which the game does only when
        the fight allows; alone, a head follows the motion's own target.
      

      

        Every character has the same sixteen-joint skeleton, which is in the
        code (0x15210) rather than in a table. The offsets of its
        joints come from 0xE1890 and the fifteen models from
        0xE1A10, both indexed by character. A motion is the
        sixteen joints' angles for every frame, followed by the root's
        position. A flag byte per motion (0x164160) keeps the
        ankles, and sometimes a wrist, level with the body instead of turning
        them with the leg or arm. Motion numbers are the table's at
        0x15FA00; the game has no names for them.
      

      

        Each robot is a record in the program (the table at
        0x19360) with three lists of parts: the legs, the upper
        body and the weapons. A motion is a pair of records, one for the upper
        body and one for the legs, that give every part three angles and an
        offset each frame. A robot has 103 pairs from +0x70 of
        its record; repeats are dropped, and the numbers are the pairs'. The
        legs are drawn from where the robot stands, and the upper body from
        the waist. The weapon is drawn in the hand without the aim the fight
        gives it. Zig-Raht is drawn by another routine and is not here.
      

      

        The people by the course: three figures of 5, 8 and 12 parts, each
        part a model of its own, placed from the figure's origin by one
        routine (0x5CA290). A frame is every part's place and
        two turns; there are 30, which the game plays forward and back, one
        a frame. The figure a spectator shows comes from the table at
        0x5CA280, picked by the cabinet's operation days over
        thirty. The cars are bodies too, a body and four wheels, which
        0x5C2A80 turns by code each frame from the car's state:
        the front two steered (+0x24), all four spun
        (+0x2C) and sprung (+0x30 on). There is no
        motion data; what plays here is the attract mode's recorded runs, a
        car's state every fourth frame, eased between as 0x28990
        eases it.
      

      

        Every player has the same fifteen-joint skeleton, which is in the
        code (0x214A0 for the legs, 0x21A70 for the
        rest; 0x20850 for team 34) rather than in a table, and the
        explorer runs those routines. A player's sixteen models are a record
        at 0xF4B20, five players to a team, the away kits from
        team 17. A motion is the fifteen joints' angles for every frame,
        from the table at data 0x6E3CA0; the game has no names
        for them. Each motion picks the hands the player holds (its record
        at 0xF2C60); mirrored plays it the other way
        round, as the game does when a step of a player's move sequence
        (0x5A300) has bit 12 or 14 set; backward
        plays it from its last frame to its first, as the game does when
        the step has bit 13 or 14 set (bit 14 is both). In 58 of the motions
        the head turns toward the play (0xB1710), up to 67.5°
        either way and tilted up to 22.5°: Head turn sets that
        turn. The game doesn't blend one motion into the next; a new
        motion starts at its first frame (its last, played backward), as
        here. The motion's clock doesn't move a frame a draw (1/30 s): it
        moves by the speed in the record (0x80 to 0x4C0, 0x100 a frame)
        times the player's pace (+0x16), easing
        between frames as the game does.
      

      

        The figures: ten tables in the data ROM, each a list of
        frames and a count of parts, every part a model of its own. A frame is
        a 64-byte record a part, its model and the matrix it is drawn with,
        baked: 0xE1D70 pushes, translates to the place at
        +0x34, multiplies by the 3x3 at +0x10, draws
        and pops, with no parent. The game names none of them: the names say
        what they show. The four rides are the ride's own screen before the
        race (mode 5, 0xE44B0 and on): the woman, the riders and
        the crane, placed and clocked as the game does, the sled in the
        ride's colour (0xE1F30), waiting and after Entry.
      

      

        The fighters are drawn by the game's own fight code, run in the
        explorer: the attract's fight is set up with the fighter in player
        one's place and the motion started as the player routine starts one
        (0x420C0), then each frame runs the body routine
        (0x457C0) and the hair and ribbon (0x4FEB0).
        A motion id indexes the table at 0x6000010, or from
        0x552 the one at 0x6FC7AC0. A fighter's own
        moves are the ones its action scripts (0xD8000) start.
        The fighter steps as the motion does; the hair swings from the
        frames before.
      

      

        A motion id indexes offset_list_motions at
        0x6400004. Its record holds 60 channels — 20 objects
        × 3 axes — of constant, linear or cubic-Hermite keyframes;
        twelve objects are joint angles and eight are the points the pose is
        solved towards. The rig is not a plain chain: the chest and head aim at
        a target, and each limb is a two-bone IK chain reaching for one, exactly
        as calc_rob_angle_cont drives the coprocessor. See
        Animation.
